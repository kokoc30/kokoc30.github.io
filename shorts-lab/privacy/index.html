Shorts Lab Privacy Policy

Applies to the app "Shorts Lab - Psychology Neon". Last updated 24 November 2026.

Shorts Lab is a private tool that runs on its owner's own Windows computer. It is used only by the owner of the Psychology Neon YouTube channel to upload and schedule that channel's own videos. It is not offered to the public. See the Shorts Lab home page.

Google data the app accesses

When the owner connects a channel, the app asks Google for two permissions:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload: to upload the channel's own videos, with their title, description, privacy setting and scheduled publish time.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly: to read the connected channel's ID and name, so the app can check it is connected to the right channel; to read the status of videos it uploaded (processing, privacy, scheduled time); and to read the IDs and titles of the channel's most recent uploads, so a video whose upload confirmation was lost is found instead of being uploaded twice.

Narrower permissions would not work: uploading requires youtube.upload, and confirming the channel identity and upload status requires read access. The app does not request access to Gmail, Drive, contacts or any other Google service, and does not read comments, subscribers, analytics or account settings.

How the data is used

Google data is used only to publish the owner's videos to the owner's channel and to confirm that publishing worked. It is not used for advertising, profiling, selling, or training AI models.

How it is stored and protected

  • Everything stays on the owner's computer. There is no Shorts Lab server or cloud account that receives Google data.
  • The Google authorization (refresh) token and OAuth client secret are encrypted with Windows Data Protection (DPAPI) in a file whose permissions are restricted to the owner's Windows account, SYSTEM and Administrators. They are never written to logs, shown in the app's interface, or put in web addresses.
  • The channel ID and name, and the YouTube video IDs, titles and status of uploaded videos, are kept in a local database so the app can track what it published.
  • The app's web interface listens only on the local computer (127.0.0.1) and is not reachable from the internet.

This protects against other people reading the files, but not against someone who controls the owner's Windows account or administrator access to the computer.

Sharing

Google user data is not shared with, sold to, or transferred to anyone. The app sends data only to Google's YouTube Data API, to perform the uploads and checks described above.

Separately, the owner can use the app to post the same videos to Instagram and TikTok through Buffer, using a Cloudinary link to the video file. These posts use the owner's own Buffer and Cloudinary accounts and video files. No Google account data or Google tokens are sent to those services.

Revoking access

Access can be removed at any time at Google Account → Third-party connections, by choosing "Shorts Lab - Psychology Neon" and removing its access. After that, the stored token no longer works and the app stops uploading.

Retention and deletion

The token is kept until access is revoked or the connection is replaced. Records of uploaded videos are kept in the local database as a publishing history. To delete all retained data, revoke access as above and delete the Shorts Lab data folder on the computer. Videos already published on YouTube are managed in YouTube Studio and are not deleted by the app.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Shorts Lab's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Changes

If the app's use of Google data changes, this page will be updated before the change, with a new date above.
